## The Constraint

The building owner needed 24 identical kitchen units installed in a recently acquired historic brownstone being converted to rental apartments. The preservation board required that all cabinetry match the building's 1920s character — no modern European hardware, no frameless construction, no visible fasteners in period-visible areas.

The budget was fixed at $45,000 for all 24 units including materials, fabrication, finishing, and installation. The timeline was 8 weeks from approval to final walkthrough. There was no room for a second order of materials.

## The Approach

Before cutting a single board, I spent three days on planning:

**Material optimization.** I modeled all 24 units in Fusion 360 and ran a nested cut list to minimize sheet goods waste. By standardizing panel sizes across all units, I reduced the plywood order from 142 sheets to 127 — a 10% material savings that protected margin on a fixed-price contract.

**Production sequencing.** I designed the build in 6-unit batches rather than building all 24 simultaneously. This let me refine the process on the first batch, catch errors early, and maintain consistent quality without overwhelming shop space.

**Jig system.** I designed three CNC-cut jigs for the face frame assembly, hinge mortising, and drawer box clamping. These jigs turned what would have been 45 minutes of layout per unit into a 12-minute operation.

## The Build

The first batch of 6 units took 10 days. By the third batch, we were completing 6 units in 7 days — a 30% improvement driven entirely by jig refinement and workflow learning.

Key production stages:
- CNC panel cutting with nested optimization
- Face frame assembly using dedicated jig
- Hinge mortising with CNC-cut template
- Drawer box assembly line (batch process)
- Finishing (spray lacquer, 2 coats + sand between)
- Site installation in 3-unit sequences

## The Outcome

All 24 units were installed 2 days ahead of schedule. The preservation board approved every unit on first review — no punch-list items. Final material cost came in at $42,300, leaving healthy margin on the fixed contract.

The jig system I built for this project has since been reused on three additional kitchen jobs, saving approximately 15 hours of setup time per project.
