A new home-organizing business needed a site before it had a brand — no visual identity, no copy, no one voice yet on the page. The business itself runs on trust and a specific tone: warm, direct, no corporate-speak, no hollow positivity.

## The Constraint

There was nothing to start from. No brand guidelines, no existing copy to adapt, no prior site to redesign — and no design team or dev team to hand any part of this off to. Every decision, from the color palette to the sentence-level voice to the deploy pipeline, had to be made by one person and had to be consistent with a tone that didn't exist yet outside of conversation.

## What I Built

I owned the whole thing myself, start to finish: art direction, copy, frontend build, and deployment. No design handoff, no dev handoff — one person carrying the idea from a blank page to a live site that reads like a person, not a template.

## The Judgment Call

The easy failure mode for a solo build is scope creep — chasing every feature a "real" web team would add just because it's expected. I kept the build deliberately lightweight: the site had one job, establish the brand's voice and get people to book a consultation, and every decision got measured against that job, not against what a bigger site would have shipped.

## What It Answered

Proved the brand could stand on its own, visually and verbally, before a single client interaction happened. It's still live, still the front door for the business.
