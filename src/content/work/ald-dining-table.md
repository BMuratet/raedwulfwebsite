The legs were the whole problem on this table.

A straight leg is straightforward — dimension it, cut it, join it. An angled leg that splays in two axes is a different kind of challenge. The geometry that looks clean in a render has to actually connect to the apron at the right angle, from the right direction, with the right joint. Get any of those wrong and the table rocks, or the joint gaps, or the whole thing looks off-kilter even if it's technically square.

## The Design

<!-- TODO: Describe the design process — who requested the angle? What was the aesthetic reference? How did you arrive at the specific geometry? -->

Everything about this table started from the legs. The compound splay was modeled in Fusion 360 first — there's no reliable way to work out a compound angle joinery problem by hand-drafting. The model let me check apron connection angles, confirm the leg meets the floor flat, and verify the proportions before touching material.

## The CNC Work

The leg profiles were CNC-carved. Four legs, all identical — the kind of consistency that's genuinely hard to achieve with hand tools when you're working compound angles. The toolpath went in two passes: a roughing pass to clear the bulk of material, then a finish pass for the final surface.
